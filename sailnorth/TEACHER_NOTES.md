# Sail North: teacher notes

## Standards
- California HSS 4.2.3: Spanish exploration and colonization of California (Cabrillo, Vizcaíno, Portolá)
- HSS 4.1: physical geography of California (the Pacific Ocean and coast)
- Map reading, cause and effect, and using text evidence

## The big idea
The chapter says the Pacific coast was a challenge: the current flows south, the wind usually blows from the northwest,
and both made it very hard to sail north. Fog, rocky waters, and few harbors added to the problem. The game turns that
into a puzzle students solve by *tacking* (zigzagging).

## How the game follows the chapter
| Harbor in the game | Chapter facts used |
|---|---|
| San Diego Bay (Cabrillo, 1542) | Claimed the land for Spain; the Kumeyaay were afraid and attacked crew who rowed ashore to fish |
| Monterey Bay (Vizcaíno, 1602) | Six months to reach San Diego; Monterey named a perfect harbor; Spain lost interest for 150+ years |
| Drakes Bay (Drake, 1579) | Leaking ship, repairs near San Francisco, Miwok welcome, over a month's stay, the brass plate |
| Oregon coast (Cabrillo's crew) | Cabrillo died and was buried in California; the crew sailed on to Oregon and returned to Mexico |

## Please check
1. **Three voyages in one.** Cabrillo (1542), Drake (1579), and Vizcaíno (1602) sailed in different years. The game puts their harbors on one trip.
   Each card names its own explorer and year, and the last screen says so. A class discussion would help.
2. **Sailing physics is simplified.** Speeds by angle to the wind are simplified. The numbers are chosen so tacking helps and the voyage is solvable, not to match real ships.
3. **The Kumeyaay encounter.** The text follows the chapter closely. Students may want to talk about why the Kumeyaay were afraid.
4. **Native peoples on the map.** Kumeyaay, Chumash, and Miwok are labeled in approximate locations to show the coast was already inhabited.
   Chumash appears in the chapter (Portolá's expedition) and is placed on the south-central coast.
5. **The map** uses real coastline data, but the sailing grid is coarse (about 50 km per square), so the coast and harbors are approximations.

## Support for different readers
- **Easy mode:** more supplies, fewer rocks, no fog, unlimited hints.
- Number buttons and colors (green is fast, yellow is slow, gray is blocked) show speed without reading.
- Pointing at a direction shows where it would take you.
- A "stuck coach" appears when a student isn't making progress.

## Discussion questions
1. Why did Cabrillo's crew find it so hard to sail north?
2. What did you learn from tacking? Why is zigzagging useful?
3. How might the Kumeyaay, the Chumash, and the Miwok have felt when the ships arrived?
4. Why did Spain lose interest in California after Vizcaíno?
