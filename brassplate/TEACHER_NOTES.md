# The Mystery of the Brass Plate: teacher notes

## Standards
- California HSS 4.2.3 (Spanish and English exploration of California)
- Historical and Social Sciences Analysis Skills (grades 4 and 5): research, evidence, and point of view
- Reading informational text, and explaining events using specific information from the text

## What students do
Students act as history detectives and follow the chapter's story in order.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1579 | Short story of Drake's visit and the plate. The crew's description is a **primary source**. |
| 1936 | The clue: a shop clerk finds a dirty metal plate. Is it proof? (No. We need to check it.) |
| The Match | Students check each part of the 1579 description against the plate. A match supports "real" but does not prove it. |
| 1937 | **Decision:** what should Bolton do? Then a warning sign: some historians worry it's fake. |
| 1977 | Scientific tests show the brass could not have been made in the 1500s. Students give a verdict and a reason. |
| The Truth | The prank, the "artifake," and what happened to Bolton. |
| Think | The chapter's big question: *What challenges do people face when exploring the past?* |
| Case Files | Four new cases (three fake, one probably real) to practice the skills. |

## Scoring (33 points)
- Clue questions: up to 2 points each (4 clues)
- Decisions: up to 2 points each (2 decisions)
- Verdict: 3 points, plus 2 for the best reason
- Think question: up to 4 points
- Case Files: up to 3 points each (4 cases)
- Ranks: Chief Historian (27 or more), History Detective (17 or more), Junior Detective

The scoring is meant to be gentle. Every answer gets an explanation, and students can see why the best answer is best.

## Things to check
1. **Facts from the chapter.** Dates, names, and events follow the Read & Do closely: Drake in 1579, Shinn's 1936 find, Bolton's 1937 announcement, the 1977 tests, and the prank by Bolton's friends.
2. **Fairness to Bolton.** The game does not mock him. He was fooled by a joke that went too far, and the game says his friends were dismayed.
3. **The plate's inscription.** The drawn plate uses the modern spelling of the inscription shown in the chapter. It is a drawing and not a copy of the textbook's photograph.
4. **Case Files facts are outside the chapter:** ballpoint pens were not common until the 1900s, Phillips-head screws date from the 1930s, and California became a state in 1850. Please confirm these fit your class.
5. **The Miwok question** at the end is a discussion prompt and is not scored. The plate claims the land's king and people freely gave up their right to it, but the Miwok people lived there and welcomed Drake.

## Reading support
- Each clue has short text, with a "Read more" box for extra details.
- Pictures and large buttons guide students through the steps.
- Students can reopen the Evidence board at any time to review what they have decided.

## Discussion questions
1. Why did Bolton believe the plate was real? Was that a good reason?
2. What would you have done in 1937?
3. Why do historians use tests and not just their own feelings?
4. Whose point of view is missing from the story of the plate?
